Family Expectations, Political Voice

The puzzle

The old measure of ability

  • Political efficacy is usually measured on a scale: do you feel able to influence government, yes or no.
  • For a young woman weighing what her family expects of her, that "ability" may not start at the ballot box.

It may start at the dinner table — in what she is allowed to say out loud, and to whom.

Muslim-majority efficacy study, 2025

My research question

How do young women in Kazakhstan describe the role of family expectations in shaping their sense of ability to participate in or influence politics?

Fall 2026

Why not just run a survey · epistemology

Two ways to ask the same question

A survey would ask

"Do you feel able to influence government?" — circle one, 1 to 4.

Decides in advance what "ability" means, before she gets a chance to answer.

I'm asking instead

What does your family expect of you — and what happens, in your own words, when what you want runs into that?

Lets her define "ability" on her own terms, inside her own relationships.

Interpretivism isn't a school of thought I picked off a shelf — for this question, it's the only design honest about the fact that "ability" is something she describes, not something I can score.

What this adds · a map, not a bullet list

Where existing work has already looked

Every study that asks women directly asks women already inside politics. Nobody has asked the much larger group still deciding whether family gives them permission to have a voice at all.

My lens · four loose starting ideas

Internal political efficacy

Voice vs. silence

What I expect (and could be wrong)

My expectations

  1. More about implicit permission from family than formal political knowledge.
  2. "Ability to influence" described in private, household terms before public ones.
  3. A tension between loyalty to family and her own political voice.

Interpretivist does not mean having no expectations.

Where I stand · positionality

An insider, of a particular kind

I'm an insider to this question — I'm a young woman in Kazakhstan myself, studying political science and international relations. Family expectations aren't something I read about; I live inside the same set of them.

I haven't been through marriage or building my own family yet, so I don't carry that specific version of the pressure some of my respondents may describe. But as a PSIR student, I already feel how the family expectations that sit over women in our society quietly limit my own sense of political agency — sometimes before I've even tried anything.

I'm honestly not sure whether that makes me partial, too close to assume I already know the answer, or exactly positioned to notice what an outsider would miss. I'm not resolving that before I start. I'm carrying it into the interviews as something to stay honest about.

Working with AI · transparency

How AI helped, and where it didn't

  • Searched academic sources and sorted them into three layers: the mechanism, the region, Kazakhstan specifically.
  • Stress-tested whether each source actually fit an interpretivist design, and surfaced closer qualitative precedents when it didn't.
  • Helped draft and revise the gap statement, the literature review, and this page's design and code.
  • Caught and corrected its own earlier mistake — it had described one source as fieldwork when it was actually a literature review.
  • The research question, the topic, the paradigm, and every final interpretive call were mine from the start. No interview data here is real or simulated — none has been collected yet.

Not graded

What I understand now, and what I still don't

I now understand that "just asking" isn't simple. A question about political efficacy can be leading — it can quietly invite the answer I already expect to hear, without my realizing I'm doing it.

I still don't fully know how to ask about this without pushing someone toward the story I've already imagined for her. Writing this proposal hasn't solved that. It's a problem I'm carrying into the interviews themselves, not one I've closed.
