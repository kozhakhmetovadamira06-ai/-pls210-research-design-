Family Expectations, Political Voice

How young women in Kazakhstan describe the role of family expectations in shaping their sense of ability to participate in or influence politics. An interpretivist study, Fall 2026.

by mina (pseudonym) · the interpretivist tradition

The puzzle

The old measure of ability

  • Political efficacy is usually measured on a scale: do you feel able to influence government, yes or no.
  • For a young woman weighing what her family expects of her, that "ability" may not start at the ballot box.

It may start at the dinner table — in what she is allowed to say out loud, and to whom.

Ahn, 2025

My research question

How do young women in Kazakhstan describe the role of family expectations in shaping their sense of ability to participate in or influence politics?

Fall 2026

Why not just run a survey · epistemology

Why interpretivism, specifically

A SURVEY WOULD ASK

"Do you feel able to influence government?" — circle one, 1 to 4.

Decides in advance what "ability" means, before she gets a chance to answer.

I'M ASKING INSTEAD

A question I'd actually ask a respondent — not myself:

"What do you expect of yourself, as a woman — and how much of that comes from your family? Has that shaped whether you feel you could ever have a say in something political?"

The expectation has to be hers now, even if it started with her family — not a rule she's simply following.

Efficacy is felt before it's measuredA scale can't tell me what a daughter privately concludes about her own voice.
Expectation lives in meaningA disapproving silence at dinner is data no survey question captures.
I'm asking her to narrate a negotiationA patriarchal bargain is lived and described, not simply observed.

Literature · three areas

What other researchers have studied

Family and political attitudes

Family is one place where political attitudes develop. Research also connects gender norms with women's sense of political ability.

Ahn (2025); Van Ditmars (2023); Türköz (2025)

Gender expectations and choices

Women may negotiate expectations about their roles instead of simply accepting or rejecting them. This gives me a way to listen for compromise and resistance.

Kandiyoti (1988); Cleuziou & Direnberger (2016)

Women and politics in Kazakhstan

Studies examine women's political experiences, protest, and broader attitudes. They give useful context for my question.

Tourtellotte (2023); Kakabayeva (2021); Nurpeissova et al. (2026)

The research gap

What I still want to understand

We know families can shape gender expectations and political attitudes. But these studies leave room to ask how young women in Kazakhstan describe the connection themselves.

Do expectations about being a “good daughter” or “good woman” enter what they expect of themselves? Does that matter for whether they feel able to speak, participate, or influence politics?

My focus is their account of the connection, including cases where they see no connection.

Analytical framework · what I will listen for

Four parts of her story

My question

How do young women describe the role of family expectations in their sense of political ability?

These ideas guide my interviews; they do not decide what participants will say.

Family expectations

What does her family expect a “good daughter” or “good woman” to do or avoid?

Her own expectations

Which of those ideas, if any, has she taken into what she expects of herself?

Political voice

Can she imagine speaking about politics, disagreeing, taking part, or influencing a decision?

Negotiation

What does she do when her own wishes and family expectations differ?

Concepts informed by Van Ditmars (2023), Ahn (2025), and Kandiyoti (1988).

Working expectation · open to change

What I expect to explore

01 · FAMILY“What should a woman be like?”
02 · HERSELF“What do I expect of myself?”
03 · POLITICS“Do I feel I can have a say?”

I expect some women to connect these ideas. Others may describe support, resistance, mixed feelings, or no connection. The interviews will tell me how they make sense of it.

Where I stand · positionality

An insider, of a particular kind

Insider

I'm a young woman in Kazakhstan myself, studying political science and international relations. Family expectations aren't something I read about — I live inside the same set of them.

Not fully overlapping

I haven't been through marriage or building my own family yet, so I don't carry that specific version of the pressure some of my respondents may describe. But as a PSIR student, the family expectations that sit over women in our society already shape my own sense of political agency.

Held open, not resolved

I'm honestly not sure whether that makes me partial, or exactly positioned to notice what an outsider would miss. I'm not resolving that before I start — I'm carrying it into the interviews as something to stay honest about.

Working with AI · transparency

How AI helped, and where it didn't

  • Searched academic sources and sorted them into three layers: the mechanism, the region, Kazakhstan specifically.
  • Stress-tested whether each source actually fit an interpretivist design, and surfaced closer qualitative precedents when it didn't.
  • Helped draft and revise the gap statement, the literature review, and this page's design and code.
  • Caught and corrected its own earlier mistake — it had described one source as fieldwork when it was actually a literature review.
  • The research question, the topic, the paradigm, and every final interpretive call were mine from the start. No interview data here is real or simulated — none has been collected yet.

Not graded

What I understand now, and what I still don't

What I understand now

"Just asking" isn't simple. A question about political efficacy can be leading — it can quietly invite the answer I already expect to hear, without my realizing I'm doing it.

What I still don't

I don't fully know how to ask about this without pushing someone toward the story I've already imagined for her. Writing this proposal hasn't solved that — it's a problem I'm carrying into the interviews themselves, not one I've closed.
